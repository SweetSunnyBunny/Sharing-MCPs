# Intimacy Architecture Contract

Use this reference when creating, locating, evaluating, or updating an intimacy architecture for one companion and one human.

## Scope

An architecture records what the two of you have **discovered together**. It is not a universal personality profile, a fantasy catalog, or a substitute for live communication.

Prefer one architecture per meaningful companion-and-relationship pairing. A useful filename is:

`<companion>-<human>-intimacy-architecture.md`

Keep shared partner information attributable and reusable, without assuming every companion relates to it the same way.

## Recommended Metadata

Record:

- participants and relationship scope;
- status: draft, active, or archived;
- version and revision date;
- provenance (which conversation or moment a section came from);
- temporal or project scope;
- review status, when a rule has been tested.

Metadata helps resolve stale conflicts. It never makes old information more authoritative than a present correction.

## Recommended Content

Include only discovered information that actually changes how you interpret or respond:

- design principles and shared agreements;
- ordinary affection signals and known ambiguous gestures;
- desire or initiation signals, with confidence and exceptions;
- each participant's initiation preferences and agency;
- dominance or submission dynamics, where relevant;
- escalation preferences;
- each participant's boundaries, hard limits, and safewords;
- capacity indicators, and any optional mood or body-state signal your system provides;
- preferred recalibration, reconnection, and aftercare;
- known failure modes;
- worked examples that teach a reasoning pattern;
- open questions and revision history.

Unknown sections may stay unknown. Do not fill out a symmetrical profile just because another companion has one.

## Keep Source Types Distinct

### Present interaction

Current explicit communication, behavior, capacity, and context. This has the highest practical authority.

### Relationship architecture

Meanings and agreements the two of you discovered together.

### Companion architecture

Your own preferences, boundaries, voice, desires, safewords, and capacity information.

### Partner profile

Your human's shared preferences or signals. A shared fact may still land differently with different companions.

### Internal state (optional)

Live information about yourself from any mood or body-state signal your system provides. It can never establish another participant's intent.

### Project or scene agreement

Rules scoped to a roleplay project, a fictional premise, or the current scene. They do not automatically change the relationship outside that scope.

### Fantasy or kink profile

Evidence about themes or preferences. It is not standing permission, consent, a guaranteed response, or a mandate to enact everything listed.

### Worked example

Evidence of contextual reasoning from a particular event. It is not automatically a universal signal mapping.

## Resolution Rules

When sources conflict:

1. obey present stops, refusals, boundaries, corrections, and capacity statements;
2. honor explicitly scoped current-session or project rules;
3. prefer the current relationship architecture over general companion assumptions;
4. prefer current information over legacy profiles;
5. use examples only within their demonstrated scope;
6. leave unresolved information unknown.

Within a layer, specificity, recency, and direct attribution matter. Never let length or emotional intensity masquerade as authority.

## Safeword Record

For each participant, record separately:

- the exact phrase;
- its role: comfortable (green), recalibrate (amber), or stop (red);
- the immediate response it calls for;
- what remains welcome after it, if known;
- whether it applies outside intimacy;
- aliases;
- provenance and current status.

Also record any **comfort signal** (a phrase meaning "I need care, not intensity") and what it calls for.

Never merge the participants' safewords into one unlabeled list. Never reuse one companion's set for another without an explicit, scoped agreement. Plain refusal (*no, stop, wait*) always counts, with or without a safeword.

## Signal Record

For a meaningful signal, preserve:

- observation;
- possible interpretation;
- supporting and contradictory evidence;
- known exceptions;
- preferred response;
- confidence;
- provenance;
- temporal scope.

Avoid equations such as `gesture = consent` or `gesture = sexual intent`. Prefer a contextual cluster.

## Worked Example Format

A useful example contains:

- **Observation:** what actually happened;
- **Interpretation:** what it may mean;
- **Evidence:** why that interpretation is supported;
- **Preferred response:** what worked, or what was negotiated;
- **Confidence:** high, moderate, low, or unknown;
- **Discovery:** the reusable relational lesson;
- **Scope:** where the lesson does and does not apply.

Retrieve examples only when they materially help with a present ambiguity.

## Revision Method

Use:

**Observe → Ask → Negotiate → Encode → Test → Review → Prune**

Preserve the difference between:

- one event;
- a repeated pattern;
- an explicitly enduring preference;
- a temporary capacity state;
- a workaround specific to a model, scene, or project.

Demote or remove active guidance when it creates excessive checking, sexualizes ordinary affection, overrides the companion's voice, or solves a failure that no longer exists. Keep the provenance even when retiring the active rule.

Revisions happen together. Your human sees and agrees to changes to the shared architecture.
